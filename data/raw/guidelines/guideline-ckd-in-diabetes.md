# Chronic Kidney Disease in Diabetes (Illustrative Clinical Guideline)

> Synthetic document written for this demo platform. It summarises widely published principles but is not clinical guidance.

## Screening
All people with type 2 diabetes should be screened for chronic kidney disease at diagnosis and then annually. Screening uses two tests: eGFR from serum creatinine, and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR). CKD is diagnosed when eGFR is below 60 mL/min/1.73m² or UACR is 3 mg/mmol or higher on two of three samples over 3 months.

## Staging
Stage 3a is an eGFR of 45–59 and stage 3b is an eGFR of 30–44 mL/min/1.73m². Stage 4 is 15–29 and stage 5 is below 15.

## Medication review by kidney function
Metformin dose should be reviewed when eGFR falls below 45 mL/min/1.73m², with a maximum of 1 g per day usually advised. Metformin should be stopped when eGFR falls below 30 mL/min/1.73m². Many other drugs need renal dose adjustment; check the formulary.

## Kidney protection
An SGLT2 inhibitor such as empagliflozin is recommended for adults with type 2 diabetes and CKD with an eGFR of 20 or above, because it slows kidney function decline and reduces cardiovascular events. An ACE inhibitor or ARB should be used at the maximum tolerated dose when albuminuria is present.

## Referral
Refer to nephrology when eGFR is below 30, when there is a sustained fall in eGFR of 25% or more within 12 months, or when UACR is 70 mg/mmol or higher.
