# Type 2 Diabetes Management (Illustrative Clinical Guideline)

> Synthetic document written for this demo platform. It summarises widely published principles but is not clinical guidance.

## Glycaemic targets
For most non-pregnant adults with type 2 diabetes, the recommended HbA1c target is below 7% (53 mmol/mol). A less stringent target such as below 8% may be appropriate for older adults, people with a history of severe hypoglycaemia, limited life expectancy, or extensive comorbidities. Targets should be individualised and agreed with the patient.

## Monitoring frequency
HbA1c should be measured every 3 months when a patient is not meeting targets or after a change in therapy. When a patient is stable and meeting targets, HbA1c can be measured every 6 months. Blood pressure should be checked at every routine diabetes visit and lipids at least annually.

## First-line pharmacological therapy
Metformin is the first-line pharmacological therapy for most adults with type 2 diabetes, alongside lifestyle measures. Start at a low dose and titrate to reduce gastrointestinal side effects; extended-release formulations are better tolerated. Review renal function before starting metformin and at least annually.

## Intensifying therapy
If HbA1c remains above target after 3 months on metformin, add a second agent chosen according to comorbidities. An SGLT2 inhibitor should be considered in patients with chronic kidney disease, heart failure, or established cardiovascular disease, independent of HbA1c, because of kidney and cardiovascular protection. A GLP-1 receptor agonist should be considered when weight reduction is a priority or when atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease is present. Basal insulin such as insulin glargine is added when HbA1c remains well above target despite oral therapy or when symptoms of hyperglycaemia are present.

## Prediabetes and lifestyle
Adults with prediabetes (HbA1c 5.7–6.4%) should be offered a structured lifestyle programme targeting at least 5–7% weight loss and 150 minutes of moderate physical activity per week. HbA1c should be rechecked annually. Lifestyle advice for type 2 diabetes is the same: weight management, physical activity, smoking cessation, and dietary review.

## Hypoglycaemia safety
Patients on insulin or sulfonylureas should be taught to recognise and treat hypoglycaemia. Any dose change of insulin must be made by or agreed with the treating clinician; patients should not change insulin doses on their own outside an agreed titration plan.
