# Hypertension Management in Adults (Illustrative Clinical Guideline)

> Synthetic document written for this demo platform. It summarises widely published principles but is not clinical guidance.

## Diagnosis
A raised clinic blood pressure reading of 140/90 mmHg or higher should be confirmed before diagnosing hypertension. Confirmation should use ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) or, if ABPM is unsuitable, home blood pressure monitoring over at least 4 days. A single clinic reading is not sufficient for diagnosis except in hypertensive emergency.

## Treatment target
For most adults with hypertension, including those with diabetes or chronic kidney disease, the blood pressure target is below 130/80 mmHg if tolerated. Targets may be relaxed for frail older adults or where postural hypotension occurs.

## Choice of first drug
For adults with diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or albuminuria, an ACE inhibitor (for example lisinopril) or an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) is the preferred first-line antihypertensive because it reduces albuminuria and slows kidney disease progression. For adults without these conditions, a calcium channel blocker such as amlodipine, or a thiazide-like diuretic, is a reasonable first choice. ACE inhibitors and ARBs should not be combined.

## Monitoring
Check serum potassium and creatinine (eGFR) within 2–4 weeks of starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor or ARB. Review blood pressure monthly until target is reached, then every 6–12 months.

## Lifestyle
Recommend reducing salt intake to under 5 g per day, regular aerobic exercise, moderating alcohol, weight reduction where appropriate, and stopping smoking.
